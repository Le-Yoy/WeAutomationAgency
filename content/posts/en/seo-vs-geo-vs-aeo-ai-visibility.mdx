> **The short answer:** **SEO** gets you ranked in Google's blue links. **GEO** (Generative Engine Optimization) and **AEO** (Answer Engine Optimization) get your business *cited inside* AI answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews. **"AI Visibility"** is a marketing umbrella for the same goal — being present where buyers now look. The acronyms overlap more than they differ; what matters is whether an agency can actually *do* the work, or just added the word to a pricing page.

In the last few weeks I've watched agencies — including ones here in Morocco — add "GEO" and "AI Visibility" to their homepages as paid line items, almost overnight. That's a good sign: it means the market finally believes AI search matters. It's also a problem: when everyone sells the same three-letter acronym, the word stops meaning anything, and business owners can't tell substance from a sticker.

So let me decode the jargon plainly — and then show you how to tell whether an agency actually delivers it.

## The buzzword glossary (in plain English)

- **SEO — Search Engine Optimization.** The classic: making your pages rank in Google's traditional results (the blue links). Still matters.
- **GEO — Generative Engine Optimization.** Optimizing so *generative* AI engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) quote and cite your content when they write an answer.
- **AEO — Answer Engine Optimization.** The same idea, framed around "answer engines." In practice, GEO and AEO describe the same work.
- **LLMO — Large Language Model Optimization.** A newer label for the same thing, emphasizing the models themselves. Yet another word for "get the AI to mention you."
- **"AI Visibility."** Not a technique — a *goal.* It's the umbrella marketing term for being present and recommended across all of the above.

If that felt repetitive, that's the point: **GEO, AEO and LLMO are mostly the same discipline under different names.** The honest version of the pitch is simple — *help your business show up when a customer asks an AI, not just when they Google.*

## So what's actually different: SEO vs GEO/AEO

You don't replace SEO — you extend it. The real difference is *who you're optimizing for* and *how you win.*

| | **SEO** | **GEO / AEO / LLMO** |
|---|---------|----------------------|
| Who reads your content | A human scanning a results page | An AI writing a single answer |
| The goal | Rank a page in the blue links | Get cited *inside* the AI's answer |
| You win with | Keywords, backlinks, domain authority | Direct answers, sourceable facts, structure, brand mentions |
| Measured by | Rankings, clicks, traffic | Citations and mentions in AI answers |
| Still matters in 2026? | Yes | Yes — and growing fastest |

<Callout>
Here's the trap: an agency can rank you #1 on Google and you can still be invisible in AI answers — because the two are measured and earned differently. "We do SEO" is not the same as "we get you cited by AI."
</Callout>

## Why every agency suddenly sells this

Two things happened at once. Buyers changed — a large share of people now start research by asking an AI, not by searching. And Google changed — its "People Also Ask" boxes went from roughly 12% AI-written a year ago to nearly all AI-written, and AI Overviews now sit above the blue links for more and more queries.

When the thing you sell (clicks from blue links) starts shrinking, you rebrand around the thing that's growing (AI answers). That's healthy — but it means the label on the box no longer tells you what's inside.

## How to tell a real GEO agency from a buzzword

Ask these questions. A real practitioner answers specifically; a buzzword shop gets vague.

1. **"How will you measure it?"** Real answer: *citation/mention tracking across named AI engines, against a baseline and named competitors.* Vague answer: "more AI visibility."
2. **"What's my baseline today?"** Real GEO starts by recording which engines already cite anyone for your key questions — *before* changing anything. No baseline = no proof it worked.
3. **"Is my site even readable by AI crawlers?"** Most AI crawlers don't run JavaScript. If your content loads by script, it's invisible to them. A real agency checks this on day one.
4. **"Do you add structured data and direct answers?"** Schema and a clear, quotable answer near the top of each page are the mechanics. If they only talk about "content," it's SEO with a new label.
5. **"Can you show the actual AI answers?"** The honest proof is a screenshot of ChatGPT/Perplexity/Google AI answering a real customer question — with or without your brand in it.

If an agency can't answer #1 and #2, they're selling the word, not the work.

## What actually works (the short version)

The mechanics are the same no matter which acronym an agency prints:

1. **Answer the question directly, up top** — 2–4 sentences an AI can quote verbatim.
2. **Use real, sourceable facts** — numbers, dates, comparisons. AI cites specifics, never fluff.
3. **Structure clearly** — question-style headings, short paragraphs, tables.
4. **Add schema/structured data** so engines understand the page.
5. **Build brand mentions** across the web — the more you're talked about, the more AI trusts and cites you.
6. **Serve content server-side** so AI crawlers can actually read it.
7. **Track it weekly, per engine, against a baseline** — treat it as measurement, not a one-time trick.

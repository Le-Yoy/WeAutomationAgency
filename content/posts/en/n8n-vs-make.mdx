> **The short answer:** pick **Make** if you want automations live fast, with no server and no code, at a reasonable volume. Pick **n8n** if your workflows have many steps or run very often, if you want to host your data yourself, or if you have someone technical on the team. The real difference isn't quality, it's how they **count**: Make bills every action, n8n bills every complete run.

Full transparency first: at WeAutomationAgency most of our automations run on **Make**, starting with our own lead generation machine, which we then built for clients ([the full Make scenario is here](/blog/cold-email-automation-case-study)). We've also built the same kind of pipeline on **n8n** for another project. So this comparison comes from what we actually built, not from a product page. No affiliate links.

## What's the difference between n8n and Make?

On paper they do the same job: connect your tools (store, CRM, Google Sheets, WhatsApp, email, AI…) so tasks run by themselves. The difference is elsewhere.

- **Make** is fully online. You build "scenarios" by dragging modules onto a visual canvas. Nothing to install.
- **n8n** is open source. You can use it online (n8n Cloud) or **install it on your own server** for free (Community edition).

## Quick comparison

| | Make | n8n |
|---|---|---|
| **What you pay for** | Every module action (credits) | Every complete workflow run (execution) |
| **Free** | 1,000 credits/month | Self-hosted Community: free, no execution cap. Cloud: 14-day trial |
| **First paid plan** | Core, about $9–11/month for 10,000 credits | Cloud Starter: €20/month (annual) for 2,500 executions |
| **Learning curve** | Very visual, the easier of the two | Visual too, but more technical |
| **Where data lives** | With Make | With n8n or **on your server** |
| **Best for** | Small businesses, fast launch, medium volume | Long workflows, high volume, technical teams |

*Prices checked October 16, 2026 on the official pages ([Make](https://www.make.com/en/pricing), [n8n](https://n8n.io/pricing/)). Make doesn't show every amount in plain text, so check before you commit; prices change often.*

## How do Make and n8n count usage (and why it matters)?

This is the part most comparisons skip.

- On **Make**, each module that does something usually costs **1 credit**: reading an order, adding a row, sending a message… A 6-step scenario costs about 6 credits per run. Unused credits expire at the end of the period.
- On **n8n**, one execution = **one full run of the workflow**, whether it has 3 steps or 30. Step count doesn't change the price.

Simple consequence: **the longer your workflows, the better n8n looks**. For short workflows, Make stays very competitive.

## Our real case: a lead generation machine on Make, then on n8n

We first built it for ourselves, to find our own clients. Then we built it again for clients, including a company selling digital products. The idea: find the right people, get their work email, send them a personalized message, and track what happens.

### The Make version

1. **Targeting in LinkedIn Sales Navigator**: a precise search (job title, industry, company size, country).
2. **Pulling the profiles** from that search into Make.
3. **Enrichment**: a technical step to find each contact's work email and keep only valid addresses.
4. **Personalization with ChatGPT**: the OpenAI API writes the opening line of each email from the profile (role, company, industry).
5. **Sending** the emails and follow-ups, straight from Make.
6. **Tracking**: a few key numbers watched at every step, to see where the pipeline loses contacts and what gets replies.

**The results:** up to **2,000 people contacted per day** and a **reply rate of about 5.5%**, when 2026 studies put the B2B market average around 2–3.5% ([lemlist](https://lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-response-rate), [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-is-a-good-benchmark-for-reply-rates-in-cold-outreach)). Anyone who does outbound knows that's a very good rate. And behind it, more clients signed than classic prospecting brings.

### The n8n version

For another project we built the pipeline on n8n. And since n8n doesn't bill per step, we could go much further:

- **Hosted on our own server**: no credit bill, so as many steps as we want on every contact, and the data stays with us.
- **Several sources instead of one**: Sales Navigator, plus company websites, Google Maps and directories, merged and deduplicated in a real database (not a Google Sheet that chokes at 50,000 rows).
- **Deeper personalization**: before writing, the AI reads the company's website and the prospect's latest LinkedIn posts. The opening line talks about what they actually do, not just their job title.
- **AI screening before sending**: each profile gets a fit score against the offer, and only the best are contacted.
- **Multichannel**: email, then a LinkedIn message or WhatsApp in the same sequence, with several sending inboxes in rotation to hold the volume without landing in spam.
- **Replies handled on their own**: the AI tags each reply (interested, later, not interested, unsubscribe). An "interested" gets a booking link straight away, the meeting lands in the calendar and the contact in the CRM.

### What this shows about choosing a tool

**Every contact goes through 6 steps or more.** On Make, the bill follows the number of contacts times the number of steps. On n8n, one contact = one execution, however long the workflow. At 2,000 contacts a day, that difference really matters. Below that, Make's simplicity wins.

## Worked example: confirming store orders on WhatsApp

A case we see all the time: an online store that wants to **confirm every order on WhatsApp** before shipping (especially with cash on delivery).

The workflow:
1. New order in the store (trigger)
2. Format the phone number
3. Send the WhatsApp confirmation
4. Log the order in Google Sheets
5. Alert the team if the customer doesn't reply

For **1,000 orders a month**:

| | Make | n8n Cloud |
|---|---|---|
| Usage | ~5 credits × 1,000 = **~5,000 credits** | **1,000 executions** |
| Plan that fits | Core (10,000 credits) | Starter (2,500 executions) |
| Rough price | ~$9–11/month | ~€20/month |

At that volume, **Make is cheaper**. Now grow the workflow to 15 steps (auto follow-up, CRM update, AI-written reply, reporting…) and 3,000 orders a month: Make climbs to ~45,000 credits, while n8n stays at 3,000 executions. The math flips.

*These are estimates from the public price lists. Meta's WhatsApp fees come on top either way (see [what a WhatsApp chatbot costs](/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-cost)).*

## Which is easier, Make or n8n?

**Make**, easily. Everything is point-and-click, errors are easy to spot, and you can show a scenario to a client who gets it in two minutes. That's the main reason we use it.

**n8n** is visual too, but you hit technical concepts sooner: expressions, JavaScript in some nodes, and above all **hosting** if you go for the free version. A server needs updates, monitoring and backups.

## What about data and privacy?

This is n8n's real strength. Self-hosted, **your data never leaves your server**. For a bank, a clinic, or any business handling sensitive data, that alone can decide it.

Mind the license: the Community edition is free as long as **you and your team** build the workflows. If you want to resell n8n as a service where your customers build their own workflows, you need a commercial license ([n8n license FAQ](https://docs.n8n.io/n8n-community-license/community-license/license-faq)).

## So, n8n or Make?

<KeyTakeaways title="What I'd recommend">

- **Just starting, no developer on the team:** Make.
- **Short workflows, a few thousand actions a month:** Make, often the cheapest.
- **Long workflows (10+ steps) or high volume:** do the math, n8n can cost far less.
- **Sensitive data, everything must stay in-house:** self-hosted n8n.
- **Not sure:** start on Make, move to n8n the day the credit bill becomes the topic.

</KeyTakeaways>

For us, Make is still the default for most of the small businesses we work with: fast to ship, easy to maintain, easy to explain. We've already used n8n for a prospecting pipeline, and it's the option we look at as soon as workflows get long, volume grows, or the data has to stay with the client.
